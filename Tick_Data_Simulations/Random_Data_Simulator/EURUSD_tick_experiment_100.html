What the gap tells you: The actual market reaches ±10 pips later than pure random (mean 9088.3 vs 9899.5 seconds). Both distributions share the same tick-size profile — the only thing that differs is direction. The heavier right tail in real data reflects mean-reversion micro-structure: consecutive ticks often reverse (market makers ping-ponging the spread), causing the price to "waste" ticks oscillating near the origin before eventually trending far enough to hit the target.
Experiment Setup

What is being measured?

We walk forward tick-by-tick through the data series and record how many seconds it takes until the price has moved ±10 pips (100 points) away from the start. The count increments by 1 per second elapsed, not per pip.

The same experiment is repeated on a synthetic random walk that uses the exact same per-tick move distribution observed in the real data — but with no sequential dependence (each tick is drawn independently with a 50/50 directional probability).
